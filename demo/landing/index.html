Portfolio redesign · 12 directions

Twelve landing page directions.

Each is a complete, self-contained landing page built with your real content — the Webol / Atrina / Quadb history, the four projects, the measurable outcomes. They are deliberately different design languages and, from the second round on, different structures too. Nothing in the Next.js app has been touched.

Tip — open each full-screen, then use the switcher pill at the bottom to jump between all twelve.

Round 1 · Visual language

A

Editorial Precision

warm paper · Fraunces serif · hairline grid

A print-magazine language: big serif display line, a dossier column of facts, hairline rules dividing everything. Work is an editorial index, experience is a ledger table.

  • Signals Taste, restraint, seniority
  • Risk Quiet — rewards reading, not skimming

B

Engineer’s Console

near-black · mono-forward · IDE chrome

The page behaves like your terminal. Editor tabs for nav, a live cat profile.json window, projects as an ls -la listing, experience as a git log with commit refs.

  • Signals Deep technical credibility
  • Risk Reads as niche to non-technical recruiters

C

Impact First

light · Space Grotesk · SaaS landing page

Structured like a well-funded product site. Availability badge, gradient headline, metric cards with attribution, and case-study cards that lead with outcomes rather than stack lists.

  • Signals Business impact, hireability
  • Risk Most conventional of the twelve

A modular bento grid of glass cards over a drifting aurora. Cursor spotlight on hover, a live IST clock showing UK overlap, dual-direction stack marquee, projects as first-class tiles.

Round 2 · Different structures, not just skins

E

Brutalist Poster

black / white / orange · Archivo Black · hard edges

Loud and graphic. A scrolling status ticker, oversized uppercase display type, 3px black rules, hard offset shadows and a rotated “open to work” sticker. Rows invert to black on hover.

  • Signals Confidence, personality, zero hedging
  • Risk Polarising — people love it or find it shouty
Best for standing out in a stack of CVsOpen →
F

App Shell

fixed sidebar · scroll-spy nav · Linear/Notion feel

Not a page — a product. A persistent left sidebar holds your identity, status and nav, and the nav item highlights itself as you scroll. Content sits in a comfortable reading column.

  • Signals Product thinking, calm competence
  • Risk Understated; no single “wow” moment
Best for everything findable at onceOpen →
G

Narrative Timeline

deep navy · glowing spine · one chronological story

The only one with no sections. Your career is a single scroll from 2019 to now — roles, projects and education interleaved on one spine that fills with light as you read, ending on 2026.

  • Signals Trajectory and growth, not a list
  • Risk Linear — a recruiter can’t jump straight to work

Agency-style. A full-bleed animated gradient hero with outlined display type, then work as a drag-and-scroll horizontal gallery with snap points and a progress track. Visual-led throughout.

  • Signals Craft, art direction, portfolio energy

Round 3 · New formats

I

Split Screen

fixed dark panel · Sora · reacts to scroll

The viewport is split. The left half never moves — it holds your name, status and CTA — but its glow and its caption change to match whichever section you’ve scrolled to on the right.

  • Signals Control, polish, attention to detail
  • Risk Halves the reading width on laptops

Career Analytics

Your career as a product dashboard. A real Gantt of roles and study on a 2021–2026 axis, before/after bars for every measured win, and a project table. All charts hand-built, no libraries.

A print issue. Masthead cover with cover-lines and a barcode, a contents page with leader dots, then a three-column feature with a drop cap, a pull quote, plates, and a back-page contact.

How to read these

Static HTML, self-contained apart from Google Fonts. Everything lives in demo/landing/; no app file, route, or component was modified.

Round 1 varies the visual language over roughly the same page structure. Round 2 changes the structure itself — E is a poster, F is an app shell with a persistent sidebar, G abandons sections for a single chronological narrative, and H makes the work gallery a horizontal scroll. If Round 1 all felt like the same page in different clothes, that’s why Round 2 exists. Round 3 pushes into formats rather than layouts — a reactive split screen, an analytics dashboard, a print magazine, and a motion-led page.

The gradient blocks standing in for project imagery are placeholders in every variation — H depends on them most, so it will only reach its full effect once real screenshots go in. Once you pick a direction, or a hybrid, the next step is porting it into the real sections and wiring it to the existing Google Sheets CMS.
